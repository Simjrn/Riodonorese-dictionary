### Notes

##### Verb conjugation

Riodonorese, like most other European languages, uses verb conjugation. This means you add endings to the end of a verb depending on how does it. This also means that if the subject of a clause is a pronoun, you can just omit it, since that information in already contained in the verb. For example:

- You sou chamado -> Sou chamado
- Tu nun yes Paula -> Nun yes Paula

Generally, verbs that *I* do end in 'o', and verbs that *you* do end in 'as'  or 'es'. But, to add these endings we need to take of the ending which is already on the end of the verb. The ending is always '-(vowel)r', so on 'chamar', it is the 'ar'. If we add the 'I' ending, we get 'Chamo', and if we add the 'you' ending, we get 'chamas'.

There are also quite a few irregular verbs - these are verbs that do not follow the standard conjugation rules. One example we learnt in this lesson is 'to be', since 'I am' is 'sou', and 'you are' is 'yes'.

##### Object pronouns

Most of the time, objects come after the verb just like they would in English. However, if the object is a pronoun, you have two options:

- Attach the pronoun onto the end of the verb using a dash.
- Put the pronoun directly before the verb.

For example:

- Chamo-me -> Me chamo (I call myself = one way of saying 'I am called')

##### Stating your name

You have two main ways of saying your name in Riodonorese:

- Sou chamado ... (Literally: I am called)
- Chamo-me ... (Literally: I call myself)

##### Negation

To negate verbs is simple, just put 'nun' before the verb. For example:

- Nó, nun sou chamada Paula
- Nun chamo-me Danyel, sou chamado Zé

##### Questions

Questions are really simple in Riodonorese, all you need to do is say it in a questioning voice! For example:

- Te chamas Paula. -> Te chamas Paula?
- Yes chamado Zé. -> Yes chamado Zé?
